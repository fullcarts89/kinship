# Stage 1 · What the mood boards actually do, and what PR #57 lost

Written before any new screen was drawn. The boards are inspiration, not material: nothing below is traced, and no graphic from them is reproduced.

## 1. The four boards, in concrete terms

### Electric Garden (primary)

| Property | What the board does |
|---|---|
| Composition | The art is **bigger than the page**. One bloom is wider than the phone; petals and leaves are cut by the top and right edges. The art occupies one corner and leaves the rest as warm off-white; density is concentrated, not spread |
| Hierarchy | A heavy geometric grotesque headline, set tight, dominates; everything else is small tracked caps. The scale ratio between headline and label is about 12:1, not the usual 3:1 |
| Illustration craft | Every petal and leaf carries a **two- or three-stop airbrushed gradient** (pink → orange → green shadow at the base; lime → forest green), with a fine **grain** that makes it feel printed, not rendered. Stamens end in **matte spheres**. No outlines anywhere. Darkness is used inside the plant (leaf bases, petal roots) to create depth |
| Colour | Six colours that only work because of the seventh: a near-black **leaf green** anchors the neons (lime, bloom pink, sunset orange, lavender, butter yellow). Without the dark green the palette would be candy; with it, it is a poster |
| Typography | The headline is **filled with the illustration** (type as a window onto the art). That single device is most of the board's identity |
| Depth and layering | Leaves cross in front of the photograph; a petal sits behind a letter; the person's photo is embedded in the garden, not placed next to it |
| Photography | A full-colour candid, laughing, eyes closed, in natural light; its colours are allowed to be the loudest thing on the screen |
| Negative space | Generous. The headline and the art each have air around them; the bottom third of the Today screen is quiet |
| Tone | Festival poster meets botanical print: joyful, confident, premium |
| Why it feels contemporary | Grain + gradient + oversized crop + type-as-image. None of the four alone; all four together |

### Social Scrapbook (primary)

| Property | What the board does |
|---|---|
| What carries it | **Real photographs of real-looking people**, saturated, candid, overlapping. The photos are the design |
| Colour | Hard **colour fields** (yellow, pink, sky blue, lilac) behind and beside the photos, cut at angles; cream paper under everything |
| Typography | A heavy soft display face with a hand-lettered rhythm; small caps labels; a script used sparingly |
| Depth | Overlap: a photo on a colour field on paper, each with a hard edge and a soft shadow |
| What to keep | Human presence; hard colour blocks; the feeling of a collection; overlap with intent |
| What to leave on the board | Stickers, stars, doodles, torn edges on everything, tape, smiley faces, the "@handle" profile, counts of friends and moments (contract §2 forbids counts and anything that quantifies a relationship) |

### Postcards & Places (supporting)

| Property | What the board does |
|---|---|
| Typography | A **chunky soft serif** in sunset orange and navy with real contrast against a clean sans: the most confident type on any of the four boards |
| Motifs | A **postmark** and a **stamp** as date devices; a ticket as an invitation device; location pins; all drawn with one line weight |
| Colour | Sunset, sunshine, ocean, sky, bougainvillea on sand: warm first, cool as accent |
| Photography | Places and food as much as people; square prints with thick white borders |
| What to keep | The postmark as the one tactile mark; the serif; the sand ground; the warmth |
| What to leave | Tickets, pins, "Following", "+3 going", stamps on everything |

### Connection Orbit (supporting)

Translucent overlapping circles in blue and orange with thin arcs: the only board with a sense of **motion**. What to keep: fluid, organic masks for photographs instead of rectangles and circles; translucency where two things overlap. Not kept: a literal orbit, counters, "12 friends".

## 2. What PR #57 translated, and what it lost

Judged against the boards, not against implementation checks.

### Overgrown (from Electric Garden)

| Lost | Evidence on the screen |
|---|---|
| The art is smaller than the type | One five-petal flower about 260 pt wide beside the headline; on the board the bloom is wider than the phone |
| No gradient craft | A single radial fill per petal and flat outlined leaves with drawn veins: a vector clip-art flower, which the brief names directly |
| No grain, no depth inside the plant | Nothing is darker at the base; nothing crosses in front of anything |
| Type is heavy but empty | The headline sits on bare paper; the board's type-as-window device is absent |
| The person is a silhouette | A dark cut-out figure with a sweat drop: the opposite of the board's laughing candid |
| The lower two thirds are a form | A bordered card for the quote, a pill, hairline rows: competent, generic, could be any app |
| Colour is timid | Lavender chip, grey-green ground; the dark leaf green appears only in the type and the button |

### Fragments (from Social Scrapbook)

| Lost | Evidence on the screen |
|---|---|
| Photography | A drawn silhouette at sunset where the board has a photograph; the one thing that carries the board is the one thing not attempted |
| One device for everything | The torn edge is the photo frame, the quote, and both week rows: a motif became a container |
| Typewriter font for the note | A cliché that reads as a prop, not as the user's own words |
| No colour fields | The board's hard yellow, pink and blue blocks are reduced to a lavender corner and a mint tab |
| Dotted grid background | Generic "notebook" texture |
| No editorial overlap | Nothing crosses an edge; every element is stacked in a column |

### What both kept that must stay

A visible source on every statement; one primary action and "Not now"; the Tell field and the two-item bar; nothing that counts, ranks or scores; verbatim words in quotation marks; nothing invented about Ben. Those are product rules, not design choices, and every new screen keeps them.

## 3. The bar for Stage 1

For each new screen, the questions the founder set, answered honestly in `03-self-critique.md`: does it grab attention; is the composition original; is the art or photography genuinely beautiful; does it feel like real people; could it stand out in the App Store; would someone want to explore more; is it closer to the board than PR #57.
