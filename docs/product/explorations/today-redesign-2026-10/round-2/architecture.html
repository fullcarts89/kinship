Tell and Ask the distinction that decides the rest

Tell writes memory: a statement becomes items with sources, after understanding and (when needed) a question. Ask reads memory: a question is answered only from what was told, every line with its source, "nothing kept about that" when unsupported, and nothing is written. The contract forbids a chat as a primary surface and forbids guessing an ambiguous meaning; so a single field that silently decides whether you told or asked is the one design to avoid (alternative 3 shows it so it can be rejected on sight).

Alternative 1 · Two places and a door recommended · PROVISIONAL — final form subject to Ask implementation evidence

The bar stays Today · People. Typing in People's field always and only filters People, immediately. Asking is a separate, explicit act the user takes; Kinship never decides for them whether “Susan moved” was a lookup or a question. On a person's page, Ask about Priya → opens the same answer scoped to her. The Tell dock stays Tell-only. Two ways to make the act explicit:

S1 · The Ask row (recommended)

While there is a query, the last row of the list is always Ask Kinship about “…” (or Ask Kinship … when the text reads as a question). Short queries keep their matches above it; a long question has no matches, so the row stands alone. One tap, one meaning, nothing to remember, nothing classified.

S2 · A People | Ask mode

A segmented control under the field. In Ask mode the field reads Ask Kinship… and Return asks. Explicit too, but a mode the user must notice and switch, and an empty mode before Alpha.

The answer, and what a result can be

An answer starts as a sheet for a short cited answer and grows to a full-height sheet when there are more than three results, long passages or several people; it is never a thread. Every result is one of three kinds, and the kind decides where a tap lands: an established person lands on their page at the exact line; a related person (mentioned in someone's memory, not in People) lands on the owning memory, never on a page that doesn't exist; a memory-only match that cannot support the question's claim is shown as a note under “Also in your notes”, or left out of a stronger claim. A composed line keeps B5's member-level provenance: “both notes” opens each with its own source. An ambiguous name asks “Which Sam?”; an unsupported question says “Nothing kept about that”.

Alternative 2 · Three destinations

The bar becomes Today · People · Ask. Ask is a page: a field and example questions, the answer as the same sheet. Clear and discoverable, but it changes the approved two-item bar, ships an empty destination before Alpha, and a question box as a destination invites general-chatbot expectations the contract rules out.

Alternative 3 · One field, two verbs

The Tell dock gains a Tell | Ask switch, so the same field does both everywhere. Fewest surfaces, but the dock's one job (capture) is muddied, the switch is one more thing in the most-used control, and a forgotten switch means a question written as a memory or a memory answered as a question: a trust failure the contract's "never guess ambiguous meaning" rule exists to prevent.

Recommendation: alternative 1 with the S1 Ask row. Search stays immediate and predictable; asking is one explicit tap that shows the user exactly what will be asked; the bar and the dock are unchanged; nothing is classified for the user. Not yet decided: the exact entry control, whether every answer is a sheet, whether Ask ultimately needs its own destination. Non-negotiable: grounded retrieval, source navigation to the exact line or member, not_found, no fabricated identity, no memory writes from Ask.
