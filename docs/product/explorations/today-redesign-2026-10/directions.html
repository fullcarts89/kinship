Kinship Today: three directions

Each direction is one visual language carried across the three surfaces: Today (the emotional centre), People (search-first, alphabetical, no counts) and a person's page (a portrait, not a record). The information, copy, eligibility and counts are exactly today's product rules; only the presentation changes. The recommended direction is developed in prototype.html.

For comparison: Today as built

A · The Living Garden "The Bed"

Philosophy

Today is a plot of ground seen at eye level. The hour's light washes the top of the paper and a dotted sun arc marks where the day is; the people you care about stand along the bottom as a hedgerow of their real sprigs (all one height, alphabetical, never ranked), rooted on the Tell dock, because telling is what the garden grows from. The one moment steps forward out of the grass. Nothing wilts, grows, or moves because of silence: the garden shows who is here and what time it is, never how much.

  • Founder approval required A light wash is a gradient (Design Direction §H forbids gradients). Proposed as the one exception: it carries real information (the hour), at ≤ 8% tint.
  • Founder approval required The hedgerow is the board-3 night composition brought to every Today. The moment's person gets an ochre root dot: a third meaning for ochre ("today's reason"), beside "understood" and "you said you'd".
  • Within D8 Every sprig in the hedgerow is the identity drawing at one size. Foreground placement is for the day's moment, not for the relationship; it changes with the moment and never with history.
  • People: the same list, but the sprigs stand on the row's hairline like plants on soil, and a one-line italic ("Fourteen people. Mid-October.") says what the page is. A count of people is allowed (People already scrolls them); nothing per person is counted.
  • Person: the sprig stands on a strip of ground under the name; sections unchanged; the sky wash is lighter so the page is still a page.

B · The Illustrated Storybook "The Letter"

Philosophy

Every moment arrives like a letter from someone who knows you both: a dateline in italic, a kicker that names the kind of day ("The day after", "A birthday"), the statement at 40 pt, the user's own words as a pull-quote, and one engraved spot illustration for the kind of moment (a laurel for a race, a candle for a birthday, a door for an interview, rings for a wedding). The person's sprig becomes a stamp. The illustration is atmosphere, never evidence: it is chosen by the event type, the same drawing for every marathon, and never depicts the person.

  • Founder approval required Spot illustrations are new graphic vocabulary ("typographic before graphic"). One ink, hairline, 7% wash, one per event type, drawn by us, no photos. Six to start: race, birthday, interview / first day, wedding / engagement, new job / home, promise (for RSN-06).
  • Founder approval required Display at 40 pt on Today (the scale's 34) and the italic "kicker" line. The kicker is a fixed template per reason type ("The day after", "Tomorrow", "A birthday"): deterministic, no model.
  • Not now becomes a text link rather than a second pill: one door, one quiet exit.
  • People: an index with drop-letter chapter marks; the live line in italic. Person: a chapter opening with a drop cap on the first line of Lately, and "Between you" set as a quotation.

C · The Playful Companion "The Desk"

Philosophy

Kinship is a desk with paper on it. The moment is a slip pinned to the page: it lifts when you touch it, flips to show its source, and slides off to the side for "Not now" (with a soft tick). The primary action is a physical button with real depth. The week's lines are tags hung in a row. People are tags on a board; a person's page is a stack of pinned slips. Sprigs sway a degree with the phone's tilt (never under Reduce Motion). It is warm and handleable; the risk is that paper-on-paper becomes "cards", which the Herbarium forbids for a reason.

  • Founder approval required Raised surfaces with shadows everywhere ("one raised surface, one sheet shadow"), a grain texture, rotations, a physical button, swipe-to-dismiss, device-motion sway, new haptics (lift, settle, flip).
  • People as a two-column board keeps search-first and alphabetical order, but halves the rows per screen; VoiceOver would still read a list.
  • Person as a stack of slips pushes toward the CRM-card look the contract warns against; the stack in this mockup is the gentlest version.
