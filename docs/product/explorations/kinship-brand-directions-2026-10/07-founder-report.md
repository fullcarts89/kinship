# 07 · Founder report: the thirteen questions, answered

**Status: EXPLORATORY · UNAPPROVED. An opinionated recommendation with its evidence and trade-offs; no production implementation, canonical approval or roadmap change.** Open `gallery.html`, then `prototype.html` with *Finalists* on scenarios B, C, D and F, then `motion.html` on Woven and on Garden Letter, before reading the answers.

## The recommendation in one paragraph

Keep everything the Garden Letter got *structurally* right and change its *skin and temperature*: adopt **Woven**, the friendship bracelet, as Kinship's identity. A woven band for every person (fixed width, id-only, never growing), the brand's own band on Today, charms for a celebration and nothing for a hard day, a rounded display face for warmth and a serif italic for the human lines, on the Garden Letter's letter composition, quotation rule, Resolve, plain rule, quiet day, two-item bar and Ask architecture. It is the direction that most literally means *friendship*, the most recognisable without a logo, the cheapest of the expressive options, and the one that can be joyful on a birthday and plain on a hard day without changing its nature. Validate it (and its typographic voice) with the two-week study in `08` before any production work; nothing in the committed roadmap moves.

## The questions

**1. Is Garden Letter too serious or subdued for Kinship's long-term ambition?** Yes, for the ambition as the founder has restated it (participation in friends' lives, not only remembering them). It is calm where it should be calm and solemn where it should be glad. In five seconds it says "thoughtful, literary" and never "my people". That is not a flaw of craft; it is the register two rounds optimised. (`00` §2.)

**2. Would a more vibrant garden theme resolve that issue?** Partly, at a cost. Bloom resolves the joy problem on the first open and creates two others: colour fatigue by the second week, and a thin line between warm and juvenile that only an enforced plain rule keeps on the right side. The deeper problem is that the garden is a metaphor for the user's inner weather, not for the friend; making it louder does not change what it is about. (`01` B; `02` rows 3, 8, 13.)

**3. Could a completely different metaphor strengthen the product?** Yes. Of the four non-garden metaphors, two fail on resemblance (Orbit → fintech and the social graph; Postmark → travel and events) and one on cost and density (Keepsake). The bracelet strengthens the product because its object *is* friendship, hand-made and kept, and because it is small enough to live inside the approved system instead of replacing it. (`01` F; `02` §1.)

**4. Which directions are distinctive enough to become recognisable consumer brands?** Woven (the band), Bloom (the flat flower), Postmark (the stamp). Keepsake is recognisable as a genre, not a brand. Orbit and Garden Letter are tasteful and forgettable. (`02` row 1.)

**5. Can Kinship appeal to younger users without becoming childish or trend-dependent?** Yes, and the documented examples point the way: a small circle, no scores, the friend's own thing as the content, visible human craft. Woven meets all four without a single trend-dependent element; bracelets have been exchanged by friends for generations and were on every wrist at a stadium in 2024. Bloom meets three and is the most trend-dependent of the finalists. (`00` §3.)

**6. Which direction feels the most emotionally welcoming?** Woven, then Bloom, then Postmark. Bloom is the most *exciting*; Woven is the most *at home*. The difference matters on the hundredth open. (`02` §2.)

**7. Which direction best balances joyful and sensitive moments?** Garden Letter is the model for the sensitive end and Woven is within a point of it while adding the joyful end; Bloom needs the plain rule enforced in code to be acceptable; Keepsake cannot fully stop looking crafty on a bad day. (`02` rows 3 and 8; `prototype.html` scenario F in each.)

**8. Which interaction ideas make the app more enjoyable without creating gamification?** Understood (a story becoming lines, each with its mark), Arrival (one signature entrance per direction), Yes with a nod (an acknowledgment in words), and Into a person (the mark stays continuous). A celebration is enjoyable and safe only because it performs for the friend's day, once, and never for the user's action; it should still be judged on a device. Everything rejected is listed in `04` §3. (`04`.)

**9. Which concept most naturally accommodates Ask, rapid capture and broader Showing-Up Actions?** Woven and Garden Letter equally, because both are open paper with sheets and rows: an Ask result is a line with its source and a band where one exists; a long Tell is the Kept card with the Understood motion; a future secondary action is one more line under the primary. Bloom's fields and Postmark's card fight the sheet-and-row architecture that Ask and capture depend on. (`03` §1, §5; `05` §4.)

**10. Which elements of the current Garden Letter work should be preserved regardless of the final direction?** The experience architecture and the Ask row; the letter composition; the source-backed quotation rule; one colour with two meanings; Resolve; the plain rule; the quiet day as a composed place; identity-only marks with a CI guarantee; the hairline row; the 26 pt gutter and the approved type scale's roles; every motion twin for Reduce Motion. Not preserved under Woven: the sprig as the mark, the engravings, the meadow, the sky, the sundial. (`00` §2 row 10; `06` BD-2…BD-5.)

**11. Which design is most viable to implement before broader Alpha?** Woven (M–L, two to three weeks of view work: tokens, two fonts, one generator with its CI test, five charms, four motions) and Garden Letter (M–L, already costed) are both viable in the window recorded in the contract (after the memory foundation and the household dogfood, before broader Alpha). Bloom is L with an ongoing illustration cost. (`05` §2.)

**12. What evidence would change your recommendation?** (a) The study showing Woven reads as craft or camp to a majority, or as feminine-coded by men who would not use it; (b) Bloom winning "would you open this" across *all four* age cohorts without losing on trust and on the sensitive scenario; (c) older cohorts preferring the Garden Letter outright *and* the founder deciding that cohort is the first market; (d) floss colours failing colour-vision separation with no palette that passes; (e) a native build in which the band at 56 × 10 is illegible or the weave reads as a bounce. (`02` §3; `08`.)

**13. What decisions does the founder need to make before a production redesign?** BD-1 (the identity), BD-2 (the mark), BD-3 (the voice), BD-4 and BD-5 (occasion art and atmosphere), BD-7 (the motion set), and FD-1 (study first or dogfood first), FD-2 (the contract's three signatures), FD-6 (timing). FD-3 and FD-4 are the other explorations' decisions and this one depends on them only for copy. (`06`.)

## The trade-offs, plainly

- **Woven over Garden Letter** trades typographic gravity and an already-specified system for an identity that means friendship and shows joy; it costs the sprig, which is built and loved.
- **Woven over Bloom** trades the biggest first-open smile for durability, cost and safety on a hard day.
- **Deciding now over the study** trades two weeks for the risk of building the wrong skin twice.

## What this exploration is not

Not an implementation, not an approval, not a change to Today's eligibility, memory, trust, consent, notifications, navigation, the coverage matrix or the roadmap. No production file was touched; no flag exists; no account was looked at; the people in it are fictional.
