# 08 · A lightweight usability and preference study

**Status: PROPOSAL.** A two-week, moderated study with diverse participants, so that no direction is ever called "Gen Z approved" on a designer's say-so. It uses the static prototypes on a real iPhone in Safari; no production code, no real data, no provider processing, no account.

## Purpose

To learn (1) how each finalist is read in the first five seconds, (2) whether its joy survives the sensitive scenario, (3) which typographic voice people want to open, and (4) whether any mark is read as meaning something it must not mean.

## Participants

- **n = 24**, recruited outside the household and outside the founder's network, screened for "has at least five friends they message in a normal week" (the product's premise) and for no design or tech-product employment.
- **Six per cohort:** 18–25 · 26–35 · 36–50 · 51+. Within each cohort, a gender mix and at least one person who uses larger text or Reduce Motion on their phone. Two participants overall with a known colour-vision deficiency (recruited specifically).
- Paid for their time. Consent is explicit, written, and covers recording, the fictional content, and that nothing they say is stored with their name.

## Materials

`prototype.html` (finalists only: A, B, F and F′), `motion.html`, on an iPhone 13 and an iPhone SE, served locally; all sessions on the same fixtures. Order of directions counterbalanced (Latin square) so first-seen bias is spread.

## Protocol (40 minutes, moderated, remote or in person)

1. **Five-second test, per direction** (Today, a Moment): the phone is shown for five seconds and hidden. "What kind of app is this? How would you feel opening it? Who is it for?" Recorded verbatim.
2. **Think-aloud, per direction:** open Today (B), tap through to the source, open the hand-off, come back, answer Yes; then People at 110; then Priya's page; then the sensitive scenario (F). Prompts: "What is this telling you? What do you think the colours / the band / the flower mean? Does anything here feel like a score?"
3. **The voice:** Woven vs Woven-serif on the same Moment and the same portrait. "Which would you rather open every day? Why?"
4. **Motion:** Arrival, Celebration and Yes in two directions each, with and without Reduce Motion. "What just happened? Did it feel like it was for you or for Maya?"
5. **Preference and the five questions:** rank the three finalists; for the top choice answer the brief's five first-impression questions; "would you show this to a friend?"; "what might make you stop using it?"; a 1–5 rating of "I would trust this with private things about my friends" and of "this would feel right on a bad day" per direction.
6. **Accessibility pass** (participants who use larger text or Reduce Motion, and the two with colour-vision deficiency): the same screens at their settings; name three people's bands or flowers apart.

## Measures and analysis

- Counts of first-impression categories per direction and cohort (an app for…; a feeling of…); ranked preference counts; the two 1–5 ratings as medians per direction with the full distribution shown; verbatims for the "means a score?" probes; task completion for the hand-off and return (whether participants understood that opening Messages did not count).
- **Report n everywhere; no p-values on 24 participants; no "statistically significant".** Differences are reported as counts and quotes, and a direction "wins" a question only when it leads in three of four cohorts.
- Pre-registered stopping rule for Woven: if ≥ 8 of 24 describe it unprompted as crafty, childish or "for girls" and rank it last, the recommendation changes (`07` Q12).

## What this study cannot tell us

Whether people *keep opening* any direction over weeks (that is the household dogfood's question), whether the native build feels as the prototype does, or anything about real memories (none are used).

## Timeline and cost

Recruitment one week; sessions over five days; analysis three days. A moderator and a note-taker. Incentives for 24 participants at a standard rate. No engineering time.
